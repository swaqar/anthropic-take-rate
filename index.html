The 36% tax on the frontier

Anthropic's filing shows 48% of revenue arriving through cloud marketplaces, and the share those marketplaces keep has more than doubled in a year. Gross margin is the number everyone argues about. This is the number that decides whether a frontier lab stays a product company or becomes a channel.

The arithmetic closes to the dollar

The filing does not print a channel fee line. It prints sales, marketing and partnerships expense, and says what moved inside it. That is enough to recover the fee.

S&M & partnerships $3,168M  −  marketing & advertising $368M  =  channel fees $2,800M

Six months to 30 June 2026. The filing states a $2.7B increase in fees paid to channel partners and a $273M increase in marketing, off a base of $120M in H1 2025. Treating the residual as marketing gives $368M and therefore $2,800M of fees.

The check. Take the reported revenue, subtract those fees, then subtract compute and infrastructure, personnel, marketing and other operating expense. Why the cut doubled while the brand got stronger

Revenue grew 14x year on year and customers spending over $100,000 a year went from roughly 950 to 11,900. You would expect that to reduce the price of distribution. It did the opposite. Four explanations fit the disclosed facts, and the filing commits to none of them.

Committed spend is the productinferred

A bank that has promised a cloud $100M of consumption has a budget problem if it underspends. Buying Claude on that marketplace converts an AI purchase into commitment drawdown. The buyer is not paying for Claude, it is paying down a promise it already made. That gives the cloud pricing power over the listing, because the alternative for the buyer is to pay Anthropic and still owe the cloud.

Co-sell and private offers cost more than a listinginferred

A self-serve marketplace listing takes a few percent. A private offer with a cloud field team in the room, security review already done, and the contract on the customer's existing paper is a different service. The fee moves with the labour. As Anthropic moved upmarket, a larger share of marketplace revenue would have been sold that way.

Dependence is leverageinferred

At 47% of revenue flowing through two companies, renegotiating the fee means risking half the business. The clouds know the share. A take rate that rises as dependence rises is what that asymmetry looks like in a P&L.

Accounting, not economicscannot verify

Fees could have been reclassified into sales and marketing as the arrangements were formalised, or partner-led resale could have been added to the channel definition. The filing does not break the fee out by partner or by contract type, so a step change caused by presentation cannot be ruled out.

What the filing does not say

The gap matters more than the disclosure, because the gap is where the next four quarters of argument will happen.

MissingWhy it matters
Fee split by partnerTwo clouds with different economics are reported as one number. A 36% blended rate could be 30% and 45%.
Fee basisPercentage of gross, a flat per-deal fee, or a negotiated schedule. Only the total is given.
Contract term and terminationA take rate that can be reset annually is a different risk from one locked for five years.
Gross marginCompute and infrastructure is one line. It covers serving customers and training models. Any gross margin figure, including the widely quoted 40%, is a model of how that line splits.
Direct versus channel pricingIf the same token costs more through a marketplace, the customer is paying the fee. If it costs the same, Anthropic is.
Read the compute line carefully. In 2025 Anthropic spent $7,326M on compute and infrastructure against $4,585M of revenue, or $1.60 for every dollar it earned. In H1 2026 that fell to $0.55. The channel fee is now the second largest single claim on revenue after compute. That is the whole story in two ratios.
